title: Variants & Contrast
description: Set the palette's character and its contrast from the same seed.
---

The seed fixes your color. The variant and contrast controls decide how the system built around it feels — its character and its readability — without changing the seed.

## Variant

A variant is a strategy for deriving the palette. The same seed under a different variant produces a different but still-coherent system, so you pick a character, not just a hue.

The variants are grouped by how far they move from the seed:

- **Standard** — source-faithful schemes that stay close to your seed. Tonal Spot is the classic default.
- **Expressive** — high-chroma schemes that push away from the seed for more color. Vibrant and Expressive live here.
- **Subdued** — restrained, low-chroma schemes. Monochrome lives here.
- **CMF** — the spec scheme, which takes a second source color. See below.

Switching variants keeps your seed and rebuilds everything else.

## Contrast

The contrast level sets how strongly text and `on-` colors separate from the surfaces behind them. Raise it and the `on-` pairs shift to hold a larger contrast margin; lower it and they relax toward the baseline.

Contrast is **per mode** — light and dark each carry their own level — so you can push dark mode harder without touching light. The control lives in **Settings**.

## CMF and a second source

The **CMF** variant accepts a second source color in addition to the seed. The second color drives the tertiary family and shifts the error hue, so you can steer those parts of the system with a color of your choice rather than letting them derive from the seed alone. Without a second source, CMF behaves like a seed-only scheme.

Because tertiary comes from the second source under CMF, the tertiary [palette override](/docs/web-app/overrides) is turned off while CMF is active.

## What's shared, what's per mode

The variant and the second source are **shared** across light and dark — both modes are built from the same choice. Contrast is **per mode**. See [Light & dark](/docs/web-app/light-dark) for the full split.

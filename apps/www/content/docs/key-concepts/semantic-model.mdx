title: Semantic Color Model
description: Colors named by role, the brand pair, and the token set Tonex generates.
---

Tonex names colors by their job, not their appearance. A color is `primary`, `surface`, or `error` because of the role it plays, and each role has a defined relationship to the others. The role vocabulary comes from Material Design 3 color roles, but the result is not meant to look like Material Design. Tonex uses those roles as a portable color contract.

## Why roles instead of names

A fixed color like "blue 600" only means something in one palette. A role like `primary` means the main action or accent color, whatever its actual value. Because roles describe intent, the system Tonex builds can be expressed in shadcn, in Tailwind, in Material JSON, or in a custom agent workflow and still be the same color system.

Roles are the common language. The semantic source is the contract; target-specific tokens are mappings from that contract. See [How Tonex Thinks](/docs/key-concepts/how-tonex-thinks#agents-bind-tonex-constrains) for the handoff model.

## Brand pair versus roles

The exact seed color is not the same thing as `primary`. Tonex keeps the literal seed available as a brand pair: the unchanged brand color plus a generated readable foreground. Use that pair when the exact input color matters — a CTA, badge, hero accent, or logo-adjacent surface.

Roles are the system around that brand color. `primary`, `primary-container`, and the other roles may shift tone so they can keep contrast, coordinate with surfaces, and work in both light and dark. The brand pair preserves exactness; the roles preserve usability.

## The core roles

| Role | Job |
|------|-----|
| `primary` | Main action or accent color |
| `secondary` | Supporting accent |
| `tertiary` | Third accent for contrast and range |
| `surface` | Neutral backgrounds behind content |
| `error` | Error states and destructive actions |
| `outline` | Borders and dividers |

## On colors

Every colored area needs readable content on top of it. For each role there's an `on-` partner — `on-primary`, `on-surface`, and so on — that's built to read against it. Tonex derives the pair together and checks the contrast, so the text on a color is readable by construction. See [HCT](/docs/key-concepts/hct) for how tone makes that reliable, and [Contrast](/docs/key-concepts/contrast) for the audit.

## Surfaces and elevation

Surfaces come as a small range of containers at increasing separation — a base surface, then container levels — so you can show depth and grouping through subtle steps rather than borders alone. Each derives from the same neutral and stays in order by contrast.

## Inverse, fixed, and effects

A few roles exist for specific needs:

- **Inverse** surfaces and their on colors, for elements like tooltips and snackbars that stand out by flipping light and dark.
- **Fixed** roles, for stable accent values that should not invert across light and dark in the same way as the main roles.
- **Scrim** and **shadow**, the colors behind modals and under raised elements.

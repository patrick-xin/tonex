---
title: How Tonex Thinks
description: The model behind the output. One seed, a system of semantic roles, and an order that decides what wins.
---

Tonex takes one color and derives a full system from it. A few ideas explain everything the editor and the CLI do: the seed, the semantic roles, the generative space they live in, and the order that decides what wins — plus two things that follow from them, how contrast holds and how the result travels to other tools.

## The seed is your color

You give Tonex a single seed: your brand color. The engine builds the system around it rather than replacing it. The seed sets the tone and hue the rest of the palette answers to.

You provide one color. You don't hand-pick the other tokens and hope they cohere — the engine derives them.

## The output is roles, not swatches

Tonex doesn't return a list of colors. It returns **semantic roles** — colors named by their job, not their appearance:

| Role | Job |
|------|-----|
| `primary` | The main brand and accent color |
| `surface` | The neutral backgrounds behind your content |
| `on-surface` | Text and icons that sit on a surface |
| `error` | Error states and destructive actions |

Every role has a defined relationship to the others. `on-surface` is readable on `surface` because the engine builds the pair together, not because you matched them by eye. Naming colors by intent rather than by hue is also what lets the system travel to other tools — more on that below. See [Semantic color model](/docs/key-concepts/semantic-model) for the full set.

## Your identity is a point in a continuous space

The system is generative, not a preset. Two things follow from that:

- Change a single hex digit in the seed, and you get a different but still-coherent system.
- Keep the seed and turn a knob — variant, contrast, surface — and you get a different system again.

Your identity is a unique point in a continuous space, not a theme everyone else can also pick. That continuity is also the safety net: when you adjust one color by hand, the engine re-balances the rest so the set stays coherent.

## An order decides what wins

You can shape the system at several stages. Later stages override earlier ones:

1. **Seed** — the input color.
2. **Engine** — variant, contrast, and the optional second source set the overall palette. See [Variants](/docs/key-concepts/variants) and [Contrast](/docs/key-concepts/contrast).
3. **Color overrides** — step in on specific colors, at two levels:
   - **Palette level** — change the source color of a whole family, or add your own named colors; the engine rebuilds that family's ramp around it. See [Overrides](/docs/web-app/overrides).
   - **Token level** — pin a single role to an exact value. A pin stops tracking the system entirely; it's terminal, beating every other stage including a later binding. See [Role overrides](/docs/web-app/shadcn/role-overrides).
4. **Surfaces** — tint or desaturate the neutral backgrounds. See [Surfaces](/docs/web-app/surfaces).
5. **shadcn bindings** — point a role at a different generated color so it still tracks the system. (A token-level pin from step 3 overrides this.) See [Role bindings](/docs/web-app/shadcn/role-bindings).

When two stages touch the same color, the later one wins. This is what lets you make a precise change in one place without it rippling everywhere else.

## Contrast is checked as you build

Every foreground/background pair is contrast-audited while you work, against WCAG thresholds. What passes in the preview is what you ship — readability isn't a separate step you run at the end. See [Contrast](/docs/key-concepts/contrast) for the audit itself, and [HCT](/docs/key-concepts/hct) for why working in tone makes contrast something the engine can compute rather than guess.

## The semantic source is the contract

Because the roles describe intent, the same system can travel anywhere — `primary` and `on-surface` mean the same thing whether the target is shadcn, Tailwind, or a slide. Tonex exports this semantic source as [design tokens](/docs/key-concepts/design-tokens); from there the last step maps it into whatever token system a given tool uses — directly for known targets, or through an agent for the long tail. The source is the contract; the mapping is the last mile. See [Targets & formats](/docs/cli/targets-formats).

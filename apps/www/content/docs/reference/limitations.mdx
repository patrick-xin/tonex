title: Limitations
description: Edge cases and current constraints worth knowing.
---

A few things behave in ways worth knowing up front.

## Your seed isn't your primary color

Don't expect `primary` or `primary-container` to come back as the exact color you seeded. Tonex's roles are tuned for contrast, not to reproduce your input. Your seed's hue and its colorfulness carry through to the whole palette, but its lightness gets reassigned so each role lands where it needs to for legible text and surfaces. A seed in the mid-tones — neither clearly light nor clearly dark — is the most surprising case: both `primary` and `primary-container` get shifted away from where you picked to keep their contrast, so neither role is a byte-for-byte match of what you picked. This is by design, and it's the same across every variant — it's how the roles coordinate, not a quirk of one scheme.

The generated roles are a coordinated, contrast-tuned system. Reach for them by their job — a `primary` that reads against its surface — rather than as a place to find your exact brand color back. When you genuinely need your input to appear unchanged, that's what the literal brand color is for: it pins your seed as-is so it never drifts, and Tonex pairs it with a generated foreground that stays legible on top. Treat it as a deliberate override for when exactness matters, not the default way to use a role.

## Out-of-gamut OKLCH

The engine works in sRGB. When you paste an OKLCH color that sits outside the sRGB gamut, Tonex maps it back in by reducing its chroma at the same lightness and hue — the standard CSS Color 4 mapping. The result is the closest in-gamut color, not the one you typed, and the app doesn't flag when this happens. If a pasted OKLCH looks less saturated than expected, it was outside sRGB and got pulled in.

## Tailwind color picker

The Tailwind color picker offers the colors in Tailwind's default palette, not arbitrary values. For an exact brand color that isn't in that palette, enter it as hex or OKLCH instead. The picker is a shortcut for the common case, not a replacement for direct input.

The palette is stored in OKLCH, and Tailwind's most vivid shades fall outside sRGB. Those swatches go through the same gamut mapping as a pasted OKLCH, so picking, say, `yellow-500` seeds the in-gamut approximation rather than Tailwind's exact value. The chosen swatch is a starting point, not a guaranteed byte-for-byte match.

## Near grey seeds

A seed with almost no color has little hue to work with. Controls that act on hue have nothing to adjust and may turn off — there's no hue in a grey to shift. The system still generates; it just works from tone alone. Charts handle this by falling back to a set hue so the series still has color.
